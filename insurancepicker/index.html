Good to know

Questions people ask

Do I actually need private insurance in Vienna?

No. ÖGK covers doctors with a contract, emergencies, necessary operations in public hospitals and most prescriptions. Private insurance buys comfort, choice and sometimes speed: private doctors, a better hospital room and your pick of surgeon.

If you rarely see private doctors and don’t mind a public ward, keeping ÖGK and paying as you go is usually cheapest.

How does paying a private doctor (Wahlarzt) work?

You pay the doctor, then send the bill to ÖGK. ÖGK refunds 80% of what it would have paid a contract doctor, which is usually a small part of a private fee.

Then you send the bill and ÖGK’s statement to your private insurer. Generali pays 100% of what’s left if ÖGK paid something, or 80% if ÖGK paid nothing, up to the yearly limit. Our numbers use 80% to stay on the safe side.

Does private hospital cover get me surgery faster?

It can help, because private hospitals and private-class beds often have more flexible scheduling. But no insurer guarantees a date. Ask the surgeon and hospital for real dates before you rely on it.

What’s the most I could pay for a big operation?

With Generali Premium in a Vienna contract hospital: €300 a day for at most 5 days a year, so €1,500. Generali confirmed there’s no euro limit on what they pay there. A few things can push your share higher:

A stay over New Year falls into two calendar years, so you can hit the €1,500 twice. A single room costs €371 a day in Vienna. At a non-contract hospital, Generali pays only fixed tariff amounts, so check the hospital first.

What about very expensive medicines?

This is the biggest gap. Austrian plans cap prescription medicines at €415 a year (Generali), €685 (Generali’s higher doctor tier) or €519–€1,038 (Wiener Städtische). Generali confirmed the cap applies even to a €100,000 drug that ÖGK won’t pay.

Talk to ÖGK first: it has its own approval routes for expensive drugs. International plans like Cigna and Allianz Care cover prescriptions much more broadly, but you need to be eligible and should get written confirmation for your specific drug.

I have a health condition. Can I still get covered?

Usually yes, but read this carefully. According to Generali: treatment that started before the contract is never covered. A condition that existed before but needs treatment later is covered if you declared it in writing and Generali didn’t exclude it in writing.

You must answer the health questions honestly. Your final price and any exclusions are only set after the insurer has seen your answers.

Am I covered abroad?

Generali’s Premium covers public hospitals elsewhere in Europe (your excess may apply). Outside Europe, only if the treatment can’t be done as well in Austria, a doctor recommends it, and Generali approves it in advance.

On trips up to 6 weeks, all three Generali plans include travel cover up to €250,000 for sudden illness or accidents.

Are experimental or off-label treatments covered?

Experimental treatments aren’t covered by Generali, Cigna or Allianz Care. Off-label use needs a case-by-case decision. A high limit or a hospital stay doesn’t change that, so ask for a written answer first.

Will my premium go up?

Expect it to. Over the last five years Generali’s tariffs rose on average 3.74% a year (hospital), 6.08% (doctor cover), 7.14% (higher doctor tier), 5.70% (hospital cash) and 1.24% (accident-only hospital). The 5% partner discount ends after year three.

Switching insurer later usually means a new health check, and new conditions may then be excluded.

What if I stay healthy and never claim?

You keep paying premiums; that’s how insurance works. Generali’s doctor cover can refund 15% of its premium after a claim-free year (about €82). It’s conditional, so we leave it out of the numbers.

What about couples and children?

Prices here are per adult. Generali gives couples 5% off for the first three years, already included in the prices shown. Each person is priced by age, so a partner’s price will differ a little. Children need their own quote.

Before you sign

Six things to check

  1. Tell them everything

    Answer the health questions fully. Leaving something out can cost you a claim later.

  2. Get the final offer in writing

    The quote is an estimate until the insurer has reviewed your health. Check the price and any exclusions.

  3. Check your hospitals

    Make sure the hospitals and surgeons you’d use are contract partners. Others pay only tariff amounts.

  4. Ask about price rises

    Ask what you’ll pay after discounts end, and when you can first cancel. At Generali that’s the end of year three.

  5. Get cost approval first

    Before a planned treatment, ask for a written cost approval (Kostenübernahme). It’s required for direct billing.

  6. Don’t cancel too early

    Keep any existing cover until the new policy is actually issued.
