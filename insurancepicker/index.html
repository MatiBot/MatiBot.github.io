05

Make the decision

If your priority is private appointments

Start with your likely eligible annual spending, not the plan's maximum. With no ÖGK refund and 80% reimbursement, approximate spending needed to recover the outpatient premium is €682 for Generali 1AJ, €1,921 for Wiener Classic and €2,782 for Wiener Premium, provided the relevant limit is still available. These are arithmetic thresholds, not predictions. If ÖGK already refunds part of your bill, compare the private policy against what you would otherwise pay after that refund.

At €1,000 of eligible bills and no ÖGK refund, the full Generali package costs €2,326.64 including the remaining €200 bill, versus €1,000 self pay. Its hospital and other benefits must justify the extra cost. If a standalone 1AJ offer were available at the quoted component price, it would pay €800: premium plus the remaining bill is about €746, a €254 reduction versus self pay. Classic costs about €1,737 and Premium €2,426 in the same example. Higher allowances become useful with higher covered spending, rather than simply because the allowance exists.

If your priority is earlier private surgery

Ask whether access to your preferred covered hospital and surgeon is worth approximately €1,406/year for the quoted hospital component, plus the relevant deductible when used. A three day eligible Vienna twin room stay would add €900; five or more deductible days in the same year add at most €1,500. You can already pay a specialist privately without insurance. The additional hospital benefit is funding a potentially large private operation bill.

If your priority is a major treatment bill

Generali 1AJ and Wiener outpatient plans do not meaningfully protect against the hypothetical €100,000 medicine bill. Keep Cigna and Allianz on a separate shortlist until an Austrian quote and a written coverage explanation establish the premium, maximum personal exposure and whether the medicine scenario is actually covered. Buying much broader insurance solely to improve reimbursement of €1,000 of annual visits is unlikely to justify several thousand euros of additional premium.

The few answers needed before choosing

  • Obtain revised Generali prices for hospital only, outpatient only and both, including any lost discounts and underwriting terms.
  • Confirm the hospitals and surgeons you would actually use, any appointment timing benefit, and how day clinic surgery, implants and noncontract treatment are paid.
  • For Cigna and Allianz, obtain an Austrian quote with the required outpatient module, an explicit deductible and cost sharing maximum, and a clear answer on expat or home country eligibility.
  • Ask how they would handle an approved but expensive outpatient medicine refused by ÖGK, including medical necessity, off label use, prior approval and ongoing renewals.
  • Choose separately how much you will pay each year for private hospital access and how much routine care you prefer to fund yourself.

Provisional choice: for €1,000 of routine annual visits, self pay beats the full Generali proposal and both Wiener examples. Request an actual outpatient-only quote if routine savings are the goal. If private surgery is the goal, compare the full package against a revised hospital-only offer and the deductible you can fund. The international plans remain an unresolved major-risk decision.
